# Candidates

Twelve one-sentence pitches were drafted and duelled pairwise on the bounty
judge's criteria: how funny the invention is, how clever the fix is, and how
original the idea is. Every candidate is a single sentence within the
220-character gate (counts verified by script).

## Winner (186 characters)

Introducing InkHostage, a printer add-on that negotiates calmly with your color cartridges until they release the black-and-white document they are holding hostage over an empty magenta.

## Runner-up (193 characters)

Introducing ThirdTime, the USB plug that politely refuses your first two insertion attempts on purpose, so the third one, the one that always worked, now feels like a ceremony instead of a bug.

## Why the winner won

The printer that refuses a black-and-white document over an empty magenta is
one of the most universally rage-inducing tiny annoyances there is, and the
hostage-negotiation framing turns that rage into a vivid, specific scene
rather than a complaint. ThirdTime is arguably the cleverer conceit —
reframing a bug as a ceremony — but its laugh is drier, and the judge weighs
funniness first. The avocado countdown and the chip-bag elevator were the
strongest of the rest; the avocado lost on originality (a well-worn meme) and
the elevator's charm was quieter than the hostage scene.
